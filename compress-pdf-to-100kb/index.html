Who actually asks for 100 KB

A 100 KB ceiling shows up where portals want a single scanned page as attachment: certificates, declarations, undertaking forms, sometimes photo-plus-declaration combinations. The number looks generous until you remember what phones produce — one colour certificate photo weighs 2–5 MB. Between that source and the cap sits exactly one tool category, and not all of them get you under this specific line. This page exists because “compress PDF” and “compress PDF to 100 KB for a form” are two very different jobs: the first is about quality, the second is about a number.

Aim below the line, not at it

Working rule: compress to 80 KB, not 99 KB. Two counting systems are involved. Your file manager divides bytes by 1024 and rounds; portals often check raw byte counts (a “100 KB” cap can mean 100,000 bytes). A file showing exactly 100 KB on your side can measure over on theirs. Ten percent of headroom — the difference between 99 and 80 — costs no visible quality and eliminates that entire failure class. Same logic, smaller scale, for the 200 KB world: compress to 180 KB.

PDF 100KB se kam nahi ho raha? Teen wajah, teen fix

Light preset on a colour scan. Recommended preserves colour photography in your scan — which is precisely the weight you're fighting. Colour scans need Extreme + the grayscale checkbox; single text pages virtually always land in the 80–180 KB band there. Wrong tool path: image files (JPG/PNG of the document) need the Exact-KB Image Compressor with a typed target, not the PDF pipeline. Too many pages: if the form wants only the certificate page, extract that page first — one page often clears 100 KB with no compression at all, and extraction beats compression by an order of magnitude.

Encrypted files fail before size even matters

Many institution-issued PDFs are password-restricted (often permissions-only). Portals reject those regardless of size. Remove the password from your own document first with Unlock PDF if it opens with just a permissions password, then compress. And judge everything by the tool's before/after display plus a file-manager check — two independent readings of the same bytes, both of which the portal will effectively perform.

Phone workflow, end to end

The full chain most candidates actually follow: photograph the certificate on a flat surface in even light (fill the frame, avoid flash glare) → open the pre-set tool above → drop the photo or PDF → tick grayscale for colour documents → compress → read the reported size → download to a file named with its size (Certificate_78KB.pdf) → verify once in the file manager → upload. Five minutes the first time, two minutes every time after. The naming step is not decoration: during a deadline hour with twelve attachments, filenames are the only checklist that survives the chaos — "final", "final2", "final_correct" tell you nothing, "Certificate_78KB.pdf" tells you everything.

When 100 KB is genuinely not enough

Text-heavy multi-page documents compressed to 100 KB can become hard to read — and a legible 150 KB file is worth more than an unreadable one that passes the size gate. Read your instruction line carefully: some forms asking "100KB" mean it strictly for a single page, others are more flexible about multi-page bundles than their own wording suggests. If your document genuinely needs more room, re-read the requirement before mutilating the content — half the "100 KB" rejections we see are actually page-extraction problems wearing a size costume. Extract the one page that matters, compress that single page, and both problems disappear at once — no quality sacrificed, no cap negotiated.

3 common rejections — and their fix

“File size exceeds 100 KB / attachment too large”

Wajah: The file is over the cap — almost always a colour scan compressed with a light preset, or measured right at the boundary (99.7 KB rounds to '100' your side, over 100,000 bytes theirs).

Fix: Re-compress with Extreme + grayscale, target 80 KB working margin. Open the pre-set tool above, tick grayscale, download, verify in file manager.

“File could not be uploaded (size passes, upload still fails)”

Wajah: Frequently a permissions-encrypted PDF, or an internal size check counting raw bytes differently than your file manager.

Fix: Unlock the PDF first, then re-compress. Keep ~20% margin under the cap so both byte-counting conventions agree it passes.

“Document rejected: pages missing / wrong content”

Wajah: Compression wasn't the problem — the form wanted a single specific page and received a whole bundle (or vice versa).

Fix: Extract the required page first (Split/Extract tool), compress the single page, then upload. Read the instruction line for 'page' vs 'document' wording.

Common questions

PDF ko exactly 100KB ke under kaise karein?
Open the pre-set tool above (Extreme loads automatically), tick the grayscale checkbox for colour scans, compress, then confirm the reported size. Aim for ~80 KB so byte-rounding cannot push you over.
Mera PDF 95KB hai phir bhi upload fail ho raha hai hai kyu?
Byte counting: your file manager divides by 1024, portals often count 100 KB as 100,000 bytes — a '95KB' file can measure over the portal's line if it sits at the ceiling. Compress again to ~80 KB for margin.
Recommended preset se kyun nahi ho raha?
Recommended protects colour fidelity — on colour scans that is the exact weight you're fighting. Single scanned pages need Extreme + grayscale to reach 100 KB; keep Recommended for text-born PDFs where it lands small anyway.
Grayscale se text clear rahega?
Yes for text and stamps — grayscale removes colour channels, not detail. Signatures and seals stay legible; this is the standard move for marksheets and certificates under strict caps.
Kya mera document kahin upload hota hai?
No — the whole pipeline runs in your browser tab. The privacy badge on the tool page counts file-upload requests in real time and stays at zero.
Photo JPG hai, PDF tool kyun nahi le raha?
It's an image, not a PDF — use the Exact-KB Image Compressor (type 80 as your target), or convert JPG to PDF first if the portal insists on PDF.
