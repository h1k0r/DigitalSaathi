Questions and Answers

What is the difference between DPI and PPI?
DPI stands for 'Dots Per Inch' (print density), while PPI stands for 'Pixels Per Inch' (digital screen density). In digital image files, the terms are used interchangeably to define how many pixels correspond to one physical inch when printed on paper.
Why did my phone save my portrait at only 72 or 96 DPI?
Smartphones and digital cameras capture images primarily for digital screens, which traditionally use 72 or 96 DPI density tags. Even if your camera has 48 megapixels, its metadata tag may say 72 DPI until you inject the 300 DPI print header with Vytra.
Does changing DPI increase or decrease the file size?
No. Changing DPI metadata does not add or remove image pixels, nor does it recompress the image. The file size remains virtually identical (differing by only 0 to 4 bytes where the density number is written).
How do I check if my photo is really 300 DPI after downloading?
On Windows: Right-click the downloaded photo → Properties → Details tab → look at 'Horizontal resolution' and 'Vertical resolution' (it will display 300 dpi). On Mac: Open in Preview → Tools → Show Inspector → DPI will read 300 pixels/inch.
