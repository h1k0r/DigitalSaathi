The signature is the most-rejected upload in Indian forms

Photos get attention, signatures get rejected. The typical ask is tiny — a 10–20 KB JPG around 4 cm × 2 cm — and the usual failure is a phone photo of a signature on lined paper, taken at an angle, with the paper's shadow visible. The portal sees megabytes; the officer sees a grey rectangle. Both problems are fixable here.

Shoot it right once, reuse it for years

Sign in dark ink on a plain white sheet. Photograph it straight-on in even light — window light beats flash. Crop tight around the signature (no surrounding paper, no fingers). That one photo, resized properly, serves SSC, IBPS, RRB and UPSC forms for years.

What the white-background cleanup does

Real paper under real light photographs as grey, often with a gradient. The cleanup pass classifies every pixel: near-white paper is pushed to pure white, deep ink is deepened towards black, and everything in between stays untouched. The result reads like a flatbed scan of the signature instead of a phone photo of it — which is what form reviewers expect.

Getting the KB band right

Signature files are small, so the band is narrow: below the minimum and above the maximum both fail. The preset sets the target to the middle of your exam's band (typically 15 KB for the common 10–20 KB window) and the encoder fine-tunes quality to land there. Download, check the size in your file manager, upload with confidence. As always: verify the exact band in your notification, and check the Exam Specs Database for sources.

Framing: what should surround the signature

Crop so the signature fills most of the frame with a small, even white margin — no other text, no date, no page border, no finger. Reviewers compare the scanned signature against the one on your application record, so stray context around it only adds noise. Landscape (wide and short) is the natural shape for signatures, and every preset here keeps that proportion. If your photo contains two signatures or an accidental second stroke, re-shoot rather than crop cleverly: tidy source in, tidy output out.

Common signature rejections and their real cause

  • "File size outside limits" — not cropped tight, or shot at camera-max resolution. Resize here fixes both.
  • "Image is blurred / not visible" — out-of-focus original or heavy compression applied twice. Start from a sharp photo; this tool compresses once, carefully.
  • "Signature does not match" — an old signature from a different document. Sign fresh on paper and scan that; never trace or edit a signature's shape.

One rule above all: never alter what a signature says. Cleaning the paper and setting the size is honest preparation; changing the signature itself is fraud, and verification checks originals. Re-sign on paper if your style has drifted over the years — consistency between your form signature, your application record and the one you carry to verification is the acceptance criterion that actually matters, far beyond any kilobyte band.

Common questions

What KB should my signature be?
Most notifications ask for 10–20 KB JPG. The presets target the middle of that band; always confirm against your notification.
Will the white-background cleanup remove my signature strokes?
No — it only affects near-white paper tones and deep ink tones. Intermediate strokes (the pen's lighter edges) are preserved.
Should I sign on lined paper?
No. Plain white paper, dark pen, straight-on photo. Lined paper adds background noise the cleanup cannot fully hide.
Is my signature stored anywhere?
No. Everything runs in this browser tab; the file is never uploaded. Close the tab and nothing remains.
