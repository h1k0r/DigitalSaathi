SSC Form Photo and Signature Size: Limits, Pixels and How to Get Them Right

Last updated: 27 October 2026 · By h1k0r · Category: Govt Exam Forms

"Photo signature size galat hai." If there is one error that stalls SSC and allied exam applications more than any other, it is the photograph and signature upload. The rules are strict — a minimum and maximum file size, pixel dimensions, background colour, and a file format — and the portal validates every single one of them before letting you proceed.

Quick answer: Your exam's official notification PDF is the only source of truth for exact numbers. Recent SSC cycles have typically asked for a passport photo roughly in the 20–50KB range and a signature roughly in the 10–20KB range, as JPG, on a white background, with specific pixel dimensions. Read the limit from your notification, check your file's current size, then resize and convert in the browser.

Important: Size limits and pixel dimensions change between exams and even between years. Always verify the exact numbers in the official notification on ssc.nic.in (or your exam body's site) — this guide teaches the method, the notification gives the numbers.

The four rules the portal actually checks

  1. File size range. Not "under 100KB" — a range. A 5KB photo is rejected as too small just like a 90KB one is rejected as too large, because too-small files are usually blurry thumbnails. Aim for the middle of the range.
  2. Pixel dimensions. The photo's width × height in pixels matters as much as its KB. A common passport-photo size family is around 200×230 pixels, but your notification may specify something else.
  3. Format. Photos are usually JPG; signatures are JPG too. A PNG or a PDF will be rejected by uploaders expecting JPG.
  4. Background and framing. Plain white or light background, face clearly visible, no selfies with filters. Signatures must be on white paper, shot straight, without extra surrounding space.

Check what your current files look like to the portal

  • Size in KB: on Windows, right-click → Properties → Size. On Android, long-press the file → Details. On iPhone, open the photo in Files and check info. The portal sees the same number you do.
  • Dimensions: open the image in any viewer that shows properties, or upload the file to a photo app and check "image size". If your viewer says 3024×4032, that is a full-resolution camera photo — nowhere near a form-spec file.

The free browser workflow to hit the spec exactly

  1. Crop first, resize second. Crop the photo to the required aspect (passport framing) — cropping after resizing distorts the face.
  2. Resize to the exact pixel dimensions your notification asks for. This does most of the size reduction automatically.
  3. Export as JPG with white background. Keep the quality high enough that the face is sharp — this is the one image you do not want artefacts on.
  4. Check the KB again. Still over the range? Lower JPG quality one step at a time (90 → 85 → 80) and re-check after each change. You are hunting for the value that lands inside the range while the face still looks clean.
  5. For signatures: photograph or scan the signature on plain white paper, crop tight to the signature (but keep a small margin), resize to the specified dimensions, and export JPG. Signatures compress hard — they will comfortably fit typical limits.
  6. If the portal asks for a combined PDF (photo + signature in one file), build it with JPG to PDF and then compress with the Extreme preset.

Real example: A phone selfie came out at 1.6MB, 3024×4032 pixels. After cropping to passport framing, resizing to the notification's pixel spec and exporting JPG at 85% quality, the file was 31KB — clean mid-range for a 20–50KB requirement. The signature photo, shot on white paper at 4.1MB, ended at 12KB after the same treatment.

Why photos get rejected (and the fixes)

  • "File size not in range" — you are either under the minimum (too-small = blurry file) or over the maximum. Re-export at a different JPG quality; do not just keep compressing blindly.
  • "Dimensions not as specified" — resize to exact pixels. Note: a file inside the KB range can still have wrong dimensions.
  • Blurry or cropped face — caused by downscaling an already-soft photo. Crop first, and if the source photo itself is soft, retake it in good light rather than upscaling.
  • Background not white — the classic home-photo problem. Stand against a plain wall, or use a background-replacement feature in your phone's gallery editor before resizing.
  • Signature spanning extra space — tight crop, but not so tight that the last stroke is cut off. Check the preview the portal shows after upload.

Pro tips

  • Make ONE perfect master photo and reuse it across SSC, IBPS, RRB and state forms — only the KB/px target changes.
  • Keep masters in a folder named with their spec: SSC_photo_31KB_200x230.jpg.
  • Upload from a computer when possible — the portal's live preview is easier to verify on a big screen.
  • Take a screenshot of the successful upload confirmation. Servers crash during deadline hours.
  • Never share your photograph and signature scans publicly (WhatsApp groups, forums) — they are enough for identity misuse.

Frequently asked questions

My photo is inside the KB range but still rejected. Why?
Most often pixel dimensions, format (PNG instead of JPG), or background. The error message usually names the exact check that failed.
Can I use the same photo for signature upload?
No — the signature slot needs an image of your signature. Portals verify them as separate files with separate specs.
Does the portal count file size in KB or bytes?
It uses bytes internally; the KB you see is the same number divided by 1024. Trust what your file manager shows.
Is a scanned hardcopy signature better than a digital one?
Either works if it meets the spec. Many candidates photograph a signature made with black ink on white paper — that matches what examiners expect to see later.
